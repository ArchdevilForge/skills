# Visual guidance for marketing pages

Use this when a page feels templated or the brief calls for a distinctive visual direction. These are diagnosis prompts, not bans on fonts, punctuation, colors or layout families.

- Start from the actual brand and audience. Purple, Inter, serif type, centered heroes and equal cards can all be appropriate; avoid reaching for them without a reason.
- Give each section a clear job. Remove decorative labels, status dots, counters and repeated eyebrows when they add no information. Keep real navigation, version status, credits and location details when useful.
- Use typography, spacing and contrast to establish hierarchy. Let copy wrap naturally across sizes; fix actual clipping instead of enforcing a universal word/line quota.
- Repeat established patterns for comparable information. Vary composition where it helps storytelling, not to satisfy a count of different layouts.
- Keep tokens coherent across the page. Semantic warning/success colors need not share the marketing accent.
- Prefer authentic screenshots, supplied photography or a real component preview. Generated illustrations and mock data must be identifiable as such; precise-looking invented numbers are not evidence.
- Preserve original quotation wording and attribution. Cut excerpts transparently; do not rewrite a person's quote to satisfy a style rule.
- Add motion only when it communicates feedback, hierarchy or a meaningful transition. A text-only or static page can be a complete design.

Acceptance is based on the brief, usability and [visual QA](visual-qa.md), not the absence of a particular glyph or color.
